# Project brief

## Appointment

You are the Business Analyst assigned to Westmere Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust (fictional). Routine outpatient referrals are repeatedly returned for information, booking queues are growing and patients receive conflicting appointment messages. The sponsor wants a digital booking solution, but clinical capacity and safe triage remain contested.

The sponsor's preferred purchase is a hypothesis. Establish the business need, investigate conflicting accounts and make a defensible recommendation. Budget: £120,000 one-off and £20,000 annual operating cost; eight-week analysis engagement, followed by a separately authorised pilot. No production implementation is authorised by this exercise.

## Boundary

Routine adult referral receipt, administrative validation, clinician triage, booking and appointment changes. Include accessibility, patient choice, clinical escalation and information governance. Exclude emergency care, clinical diagnosis, treatment decisions and national e-RS redesign.

## Expected participant work

Produce a problem/scope statement, elicitation plan and stakeholder analysis; current-state and future-state models; evidence-based root-cause analysis; requirements and business rules; prioritisation and traceability; options appraisal with costs/assumptions; acceptance criteria and UAT approach; change impacts, transition controls and benefits evaluation. Choose useful notation rather than completing templates mechanically.

## Success

Sponsor aspiration: reduce administrative rework and time to first offered appointment without unsafe rejection or digital exclusion. A proposed local five-working-day administrative triage target is not an RTT treatment standard.

Agree definitions, denominators, baseline period, exclusions and ownership before treating targets as approved. Quantify uncertainty and distinguish evidence from stakeholder opinion.

## Decisions

Sponsor approves scope/funding. Operational owners validate working practices. Relevant control owners approve policy and safety/security interpretations. The BA facilitates and records decisions; the BA cannot waive controls.
